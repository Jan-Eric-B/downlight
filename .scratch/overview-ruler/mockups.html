Overview ruler: four options to compare

One sample document, README.md (about 3,800 px tall in A's window), scrolled to Find's current match for "cache". Every strip maps the whole document's height onto the scroll bar's track (between its arrows), so a mark sits level with the thumb when its block is in view. Mockups only: nothing here was built or measured.

A. Overview ruler, marks only

The research's recommendation. Cost: one page module of a few hundred lines with its tests, a Find listener and a painter seam; no app, core or contract change. Measure typing in Find and scrolling on 1, 20 and 100 MB.

B. Blocks shaded by kind

Small on top of A: a binary search per pixel row, so it scales to 100 MB. Risk: noise beside the scroll bar, five tints to keep working in light, dark, on Mica and with the accent color, likely off in high contrast; its slider repeats the thumb.

C. Heading labels in a wide strip

Medium cost, mostly layout and design: 120 px comes out of the reading width, so toggling it rewraps the text and moves the reading position, and the find bar has to move left. The table of contents sidebar in disguise.

D. A scaled picture of the page (minimap)

High cost: a second DOM doubles memory and needs the whole-document layout ADR 0003 avoids; Find's highlights, ticks, Sort, zoom and Full width must be mirrored; no way to 100 MB. At 6 % the prose is grey bars.

Where A's marks go

The right edge of the same window, the two placements the research names. Beside the scroll bar the marks take 14 px and can be click targets with a hover label; over the track they take no width, but the thumb hides the marks under it and clicks go to the scroll bar.
