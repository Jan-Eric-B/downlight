# 03: Copy as Markdown of part of a table

**What to build:** **Copy as Markdown** of a selection within one table cell copies that cell's Markdown, without the pipes around it. A selection across cells or rows, or into a table from outside, copies the table's header and delimiter rows and the rows it touches, and no others, so the paste is a table of only those rows. The rows come in the file's order even when the reader has sorted the table (**Sort**), since Copy as Markdown copies the file; Ctrl+C gives the order shown.

Spec: [Copy as Markdown, and a clean rich copy](../spec.md)

**Blocked by:** 01 (Ctrl+Shift+C and the menu copy the Markdown of whole lines)

**Status:** built, hand checks left

- [x] Pieces gain a span within one line: the line, and its first and last column as the rendered document's line numbers count them (comrak's `data-sourcepos` on a cell). The core copies a span trimmed of the spaces around it. A span whose columns fall outside its line or inside a character is the copy module's error.
- [x] A selection within one cell sends that cell's span.
- [x] A table the selection covers only partly becomes its own pieces: its header row's line, the delimiter row's line after it, and each touched row's line, in the file's order whatever order the view shows the rows in. A selection from a paragraph into a table, or out of one, copies the paragraph's lines and the table's pieces.
- [x] Contract: the app's sample of the pieces it takes gains a span, and the page's contract test checks its keys.
- [x] Core tests: a cell's span, trimmed, with emphasis and a link in it; a span inside a character gives the error; a table's header, delimiter and touched rows from pieces the page would send.
- [x] Page tests (rendered fixtures): the span sent for a selection within one cell; the pieces sent for a selection across cells of one row, across rows, and from a paragraph into a table; with the view's rows reordered, as a sort reorders them, the pieces still come in the file's order.

## Results

Built on 2026-11-25.

- **Core.** `Piece::Span { line, first, last }` beside `Piece::Lines`: the columns from `first` to `last` of one line, both included, 1-based and counted in bytes of the line's UTF-8 text as the render decoded it. That is how comrak 0.59 counts a cell's `data-sourcepos` (its table parser takes byte offsets into the line, and a cell's columns take in the spaces between its pipes and its text), so a Windows-1252 or UTF-16 file's columns are those of its decoded text, as the render's are. `markdown_of` copies a span trimmed of the spaces and tabs around it. A span on a line the file doesn't have, a column 0, a last column past the line's end or before the first is `CopyError::NotInTheFile`; one whose columns fall inside a character is the new `CopyError::SplitsACharacter { line, first, last }` (a span's own fields, so it can't hold a range of lines; review fix). A table's header, delimiter and touched rows are ranges of whole lines, so they needed no new code. `core/tests/copy.rs` gained 9 tests (29 in all), each piece taken from the core's own render: a cell's span with emphasis and a link, trimmed; prices ending in a three-byte character; a header cell; a cell in a quoted table (its `> ` not copied); a Windows-1252 cell; a table's header, delimiter and two touched rows that aren't side by side; a paragraph into a table; a span inside a character; spans outside their line.
- **App.** The core's `Piece`, which the app deserializes as the page sends it, gained `Span` (tagged `span`). Both core errors are the existing Notice ("Can't copy the Markdown: the selection is no longer in the document."), since only a stale or broken request causes either; the app's `CopyError::NotInTheFile { source }` keeps the core's error as its `std::error::Error::source` rather than discarding it, and the Notice text is unchanged (review fix). Two tests in `app/src/documents/tests/copy.rs`: a cell's span writes its Markdown, and a span that splits a character answers with the Notice and writes nothing.
- **Page.** New `page/src/copy-table.ts`: `cellHolding`, `cellSpan` (a cell's span from its `data-sourcepos`), `tableHolding`, and `rowsLeftOut(block, selected)`, each body row that the range from the first selected character to the last doesn't reach (`Range.intersectsNode`), as a one-line range. Reworked after review: when every character the selection holds is in one `th` or `td`, `copy-as-markdown.ts` sends its span, wherever the selection's ends are; otherwise a character in a cell counts for its table, and every touched table, not only one where the copy starts or ends, leaves out its untouched rows through `linesWithout`, as a code block leaves out its untouched code lines. The header row and the delimiter row after it are never left out. The rows are found by their line numbers, not their place in the view, so a sorted table copies in the file's order. A table the selection runs over whole copies all its lines.
- **Fixture.** `core/tests/fixtures/tables.md` and its HTML (written by `npm run fixtures`; no existing fixture or the notices changed): a paragraph, a four-row table with emphasis, a link and a euro sign in its cells, and a paragraph after it.
- **Contract.** `app/tests/fixtures/copy_markdown.json` gained a `span` sample (`first, last, line, piece`); the contract test (now `contract-copy-markdown.test.ts`) also selects a table cell, so the page sends a span and its keys are checked against the sample. `Piece` in `page/src/app.ts` is the union of the two.
- **Tests.** 11 new page tests in `copy-as-markdown.test.ts` on `tables.html`: within one cell, one with a link, a header cell; across cells of one row, across rows, from the header row, from a paragraph into the table, out of it into a paragraph, over the whole table; and, with the view's rows reordered (tables/01 isn't built, so the test reorders the `tr`s as a sort would), across rows shown side by side and from a paragraph into the sorted table, both in the file's order. One test changed: ticket 01's "of a whole table, for a selection in one of its cells" is gone, since a selection in one cell now copies that cell's span and the new tests cover it.
- **Noteworthy.** A selection that ends at the very start of the next cell or row, as Chromium's triple-click makes, holds none of its characters, so a triple-clicked cell copies its span, and a triple-clicked paragraph before a table copies no header or delimiter (tests added after review, with a start at the end of the cell before's text).
- **Left.** The hand checks.
